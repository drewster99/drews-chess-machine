Part one

The network

From board to three answers

Inside one residual block

h = ReLU(BN₁(conv₁(x))) z = BN₂(conv₂(h)) s = FC₂(ReLU(FC₁(mean₆₄(z)))) # 384 values per position y = ReLU( σ(s[0:192]) · z + s[192:384] + x )

Post-activation. The ReLU comes after the skip is added, so the residual stream between blocks is never negative. This is the original ResNet ordering, not the later pre-activation form where batch norm and ReLU come before each convolution.

Scale-and-shift SE. The squeeze-excitation unit gives each channel a gate between 0 and 1 and also an additive offset, both computed from the whole board. The offset lets a block move a channel's level for every square at once.

No branch scale. The branch is added at full strength. There is no ReZero-style learned scalar and no normalization after the add.

Per block: two convolutions of 331,776 weights, four batch-norm vectors of 192, and an SE unit of 18,848 values: 683,168 trainable parameters. With an SE reduction ratio of 6, the bottleneck is 32 wide.

The 112 input planes

PlanesMeaning
0–1038 positions of history (now, 1 ply ago … 7 plies ago), 13 planes each: our 6 piece types, their 6 piece types, and a plane set when that position has occurred before
104–107castling rights: our queenside, our kingside, their queenside, their kingside
108all ones when black is to move
109fifty-move counter, raw half-move count
110all zeros (once a move counter)
111all ones; lc0's encoder comments it as there "to help NN find board edges"

Boards are drawn from the side to move's point of view. There is no en-passant plane: the history shows the double pawn push that makes en passant possible.

Early in a game, history frames that don't exist yet are left all zero. That is lc0's default (HistoryFill = fen_only): only a game that started from a set-up position gets its missing frames filled, with copies of the first position. always fills every game that way; no never does.

This network uses the oldest input format, INPUT_CLASSICAL_112_PLANE. lc0 0.32.1 also reads newer formats that canonicalize the board: once neither side can castle, the board is mirrored left to right so our king is on the e–h side, and with no pawns left it is also flipped top to bottom and transposed, putting our king in one fixed eighth of the board. History stops at the last change of castling rights. Networks trained on those formats get positions that differ only by symmetry collapsed into one input.

The heads

Policy

Two 3×3 convolutions: 192 → 192 with batch norm and ReLU, then 192 → 80 with a bias and no activation. Only the first 73 planes are read; with 64 squares they give 4,672 slots (56 queen-style, 8 knight, 9 underpromotion moves per square), and a fixed table gathers the 1,858 that can ever be legal. A queen promotion is an ordinary queen-style move here.

Value

A 1×1 convolution to 32 channels with batch norm and ReLU, flattened to 2,048, a 128-unit layer with ReLU, then 3 logits through a softmax: win, draw and loss for the side to move.

Moves left

A 1×1 convolution to 8 channels, flattened to 512, a 128-unit layer, then one output kept non-negative by a final ReLU: the predicted number of plies until the game ends. Search uses it to prefer quicker wins and slower losses.

How the weights are stored

The file is a Protocol Buffers message (pblczero.Net, defined in lczero-common/proto/net.proto). Each layer carries a float32 minimum, a float32 maximum and one unsigned 16-bit integer per value:

value = min + q / 65535 × (max − min)

That is fixed-point quantization: 65,536 evenly spaced levels across each layer's own range. Precision is absolute, so a weight far smaller than the layer's largest gets few significant bits. Lc0 expands every value to float when it loads the file.

The field named bn_stddivs actually holds variances: the loader computes γ / √(variance + 10⁻⁵) and folds batch norm into the convolution weights and biases, so at run time each convolution is a plain convolution plus bias.

In practice the storage is closer to bf16 than to fp16. Batch-norm folding rescales each output channel uniformly, so it does not change these bit counts.

A pre-activation tower leaves the stem without an activation, since each block starts with batch norm and its activation, and ends the tower with one more batch norm and activation for the heads; a post-activation tower does the reverse. Multiply-adds count convolutions and fully connected layers for one position. "Whole board seen" is the point where every square's features can depend on every other square: a 3×3 convolution reaches one square further, a 9×9 four squares, and corner to corner is seven. Lc0's Metal backend precision is from my reading of its defaults, not measured here.

Deep and narrow

Lc0 spends its budget on depth: 30 small convolutions in the tower, each adding one square of reach, with a nonlinearity after every one. A 3×3 convolution at 192 channels holds 331,776 weights.

Shallow and wide-kerneled

DCM's live model spends it on reach: six 9×9 convolutions, each holding 1,327,104 weights, four times one of Lc0's. It sees the whole board after one convolution but applies far fewer nonlinear steps.

DCM has trained this block before, minus one detail

DCM's early presets v3_8block_3x3 and v3_16block_3x3 used exactly Lc0's ordering: post-activation, ReLU after the add, SE before the add, no ReZero. The difference was the SE style: attenuate_only (scale without the shift) at ratio 4, at 128 channels. An "Lc0-shaped" DCM model is that recipe with scale_and_bias, 192 channels, 15 blocks, ratio 6 and a 32-channel value convolution. By the same counting as above, that build would have about 10.6 million parameters; it has not been built.

Part three

What DCM might borrow

Hypotheses, not findings. Each is something Lc0 does that DCM doesn't, with what it would cost and how to test it. DCM plays without search, so anything that only pays off inside a search tree is left out.

IdeaWhy it might helpCost and how to test
spatial policy headLc0 gives its policy head two 3×3 convolutions, about 30 M multiply-adds, 4.4% of its forward pass. DCM's live head is two 1×1 convolutions, about 1.7 M, 0.3%. A 1×1 head scores each square's moves from that square's features alone; a 3×3 head can compare neighbouring squares before deciding. With no search, the policy is DCM's whole move choice.A kernel-size option for the policy head. Paired corpus-replay runs from the same seed; compare puzzle Elo and top-1 accuracy.
a fixed mask of impossible movesLc0 computes 80 planes and keeps the 1,858 slots that can ever be legal. DCM computes all 4,864 and masks per position on the CPU. By my count about 1,880 of DCM's slots can ever be legal (Lc0's 1,858 plus 22 queen-promotion slots), so about 61% never can. The compute is small, but the training loss's illegal-mass term keeps spending gradient pushing those slots down.A constant mask in the training graph, applied before the illegal-mass term. Check that the illegal-mass number drops at once and the policy loss is unchanged.
an all-ones planeDCM's convolutions pad with zeros and no DCM input plane is constant. A plane of ones turns into zeros exactly in the padding, so the first layer can tell edge squares apart. DCM's castling planes are all ones while castling rights last, so its edge signal may fade once castling rights are gone, which is every endgame.A one-plane input variant. Compare endgame-theme puzzle scores against today's encoding.
history with repetition flagsLc0 sees 8 positions, each with its own repetition flag. DCM's full10ply200 already stacks 10 plies of history but without repetition flags per frame, and the live model uses single-position basic24.Existing encodings can be compared now; a per-frame repetition flag would be a new encoding.
symmetry canonicalizationWith no castling rights, a position and its left-right mirror are the same problem; with no pawns, eight board symmetries are. Collapsing them shows the network fewer distinct positions to learn.Apply the transform in the encoder and invert it on the policy. A replay run should reach the same loss in fewer steps if it works.

Left out: LINEAR16 storage (DCM stores float32 and has no file-size problem), and anything whose value comes from search.
