Head health in long runs: nt8y vs qeu8 vs v5

value-headpolicy-headnumericshistory

Question

nt8y flattened around 1600 by 250–300k steps while qeu8 and v5 kept climbing. Were nt8y's heads failing in that window, and how did the climbing runs' heads look?

Setup

Runs
nt8y, qeu8, v5 (see E-0012), all trained before logit centering and fp32 head tails.
Sources
Dashboard CSV rows averaged per step window: vLoss (training-batch value cross-entropy, slightly label-smoothed; ln 3 = 1.099 is a uniform guess, ~0.82 the base rate) and pLogit_mean / pLogit_peak (the probes' mean and peak max |policy logit| per checkpoint, averaged over the window). Head offsets measured directly by the 2026-09-28 bf16 survey on each line's checkpoints (fp64 vs bf16 forward pass).

Interpretation

Value: qeu8's training value CE rose past the base rate from ~200k and v5's from ~250k (near-uniform from ~350k), yet both kept improving (qeu8 1600 → 1656, v5 1617 → 1720); the survey found large shared value offsets on both. nt8y's value head looked healthy in training, though the survey found its offset growing (−105, then −236 at its end). Policy: nt8y's large max |logit| was not a shared offset (legal-logit mean +1.4, bf16 KL 7e-6), so bf16 did not cost it resolution. The climbing runs did develop the offset: v5's legal logits reached −178 … −266 from ~705k, where bf16 ties up to 63% of top-2 choices, and v5 fell from ~1720 to 1557 by 800–860k. Something other than head numerics limited nt8y; its size (1.53M) is the obvious candidate but is not tested here.

Takeaway

nt8y's ~1600 plateau is not explained by its heads' numerics: its policy logits stayed bf16-safe, while qeu8 and v5 kept climbing with value heads worse than the base rate and later with policy offsets that broke bf16.
