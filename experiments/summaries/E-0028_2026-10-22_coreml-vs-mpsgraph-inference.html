Core ML vs MPSGraph inference speed

inferencecoremlperformance

Question

The owner asked for a fair test of Core ML inference against our MPSGraph forward pass: same weights, same positions, each Core ML compute unit, at batch 1 (Lichess bot, --uci), 64–256 (self-play and arena ticks) and up to 1024.

Setup

Runs / models
Four bf16 models:
  • 20260710-1-amlg: mini, 0.24M parameters, trained.
  • 20261008-4-Ew5t: zlra shape, 3 × 128, 9×9, 8.27M.
  • 20261022-9-Hhuy: the v4_5block_7x7 preset, 8.45M.
  • 20261022-2-PswQ: 10 × 256, 3×3, 12.9M.
The last three are untrained, because timing doesn't depend on weights. Each model also has a float32 copy, and fp32, mirror and fp16 Core ML exports.
Build
2607 Release (git a74e2c8f plus the benchmark code), Apple M5 Max, macOS 27.2.
Data
The 4,635 bundled test-set positions (lichess-200 + lichess-wide).
Method
--coreml-benchmark:
  • One process times from encoded boards to every output copied into the caller's buffers.
  • Every Core ML arm passes a parity check first.
  • Each cell gets an untimed warm-up, then 5 rotated rounds of 1.5 s.
  • Grid: batch sizes 1–1024 × in-flight 1 and 4.
  • Core ML's planned device for each op is recorded (MLComputePlan).

  • Placement: under cpu-gpu, Core ML ran the mini and 10 × 256 nets on the CPU. The fp16 exports went 95–98% to the Neural Engine on the three larger nets.
  • Parity: the fp32 exports matched exactly (100% same top move). Mirror and fp16 matched 96.4–99.6%.
  • Four overlapping streams: MPSGraph bf16 gained +36–52% at batch 64 and +7–20% at batch 256.

Interpretation

MPSGraph's lead at self-play sizes comes from bf16. At equal fp32 math, Core ML on the GPU runs at 0.86–0.99× of MPSGraph. Core ML's fp16 is the nearest substitute for bf16, and it is slower.

The Neural Engine saturates early, at 14.5–51M positions/hour depending on the shape; 9×9 kernels suit it worst. It wins only at batch 1, where it saves 1–2 ms per chess move.

Core ML's device placement isn't guaranteed: cpu-gpu put a 12.9M-parameter net on the CPU, 28× slower than MPSGraph.

This was one run per model on one machine, with thermal state "fair" in most cells. Up to 30% of a model's cells varied by more than 1.25× between rounds, so only gaps of 1.26× or more are claimed. Power was not measured.

Takeaway

Keep MPSGraph bf16 for self-play: it is the fastest engine at batch 128 and up on every real-size model (Core ML at most 0.79× at batch 256), the Neural Engine only wins at batch 1 (0.42–0.54 ms vs 1.38–2.40 ms, irrelevant per chess move), and four overlapping MPSGraph streams gain more (+36–52% at batch 64) than any engine swap.
