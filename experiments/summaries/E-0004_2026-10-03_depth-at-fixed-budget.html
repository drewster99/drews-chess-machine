Depth at a fixed budget: 1 wide block vs 3 vs 22 narrow

architecturedepthspeed

Question

R7/R8 (3 blocks @128) was the best shape so far. Spend the same parameters and compute on one wide block or many narrow ones: which uses the budget best?

Setup

Runs
fatty R13, 1 × [7×7+7×7] @216 (run 20261004-10-YpxP); skinny R14, 22 × [7×7+7×7] @48 (run 20261004-11-e7dM, stopped by the owner at 1,605); baseline Avg(R7,R8), 3 × @128.
Budget
trainable parameters: fatty 5,064,751, R7/R8 5,167,983, skinny 5,193,103; MACs per position: fatty 315.7M, R7/R8 322.3M, skinny 323.6M.
Build
2275 (de0f22b), with a parameters.json byte-identical to R7/R8's.
Training
Corpus replay of 20260624-192615-w3aA5b (the first 20.9M games of the Lichess May 2026 standard rated dump), batch 4,096, LR cycle 0.1 ↔ 0.001 (20,000-step period after a 1,000-step warmup, each peak slightly lower than the last), 33,000-step limit, --policy-tail-precision fp32_from_pre_bn.
Measure
pElo and NLL on the --probe-set wide Lichess puzzle probes, every 1,000 steps; both measure the policy only. Seeds of one recipe differ by up to ~25 pElo (R7 and R8: 22.5 over 29–33k).

Interpretation

Three blocks beat one wide block at this budget: over 29–33k the 3-block net is 143.5 pElo ahead (153.5 at the 33k probe). The single block is also wider (216 vs 128), so this is not depth alone, and it is one seed per shape. The 22-block net was slowest to learn per step, has ~2.3× R7/R8's activation elements per position, and ran 4.1× slower per step than fatty (3.1× while three trainers shared the GPU), consistent with conv efficiency falling with channel width (not measured directly).

Takeaway

At R7/R8's parameter budget, three blocks beat one wide block by about 140 pElo; 22 narrow blocks learned slowest and ran ~4× slower per step than the wide block.
