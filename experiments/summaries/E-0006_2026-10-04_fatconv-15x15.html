Fatconv: one block of whole-board 15×15 convolutions

architecturekernel-size

Question

On an 8×8 board a 15×15 kernel reaches every square from every square. Is one block of whole-board convolutions at narrow width a better use of a single block than partial-board 7×7 at wide width?

Setup

Runs
R16 fatconv, 15×15 stem + 1 × [15×15 + 15×15] @98 (run 20261004-15-Pm6B, fresh 20261004-14-3Rkc); comparators fatty, slim-neck fatty, Avg(R7,R8).
Budget
5,140,071 trainable parameters, 320.6M MACs per position, of which 71% multiply padding (92.4M useful; 72% within the 15×15 layers).
Build
2275.
Training
Corpus replay of 20260624-192615-w3aA5b (the first 20.9M games of the Lichess May 2026 standard rated dump), batch 4,096, LR cycle 0.1 ↔ 0.001 (20,000-step period after a 1,000-step warmup, each peak slightly lower than the last), 33,000-step limit, --policy-tail-precision fp32_from_pre_bn.
Measure
pElo and NLL on the --probe-set wide Lichess puzzle probes, every 1,000 steps; both measure the policy only. Seeds of one recipe differ by up to ~25 pElo (R7 and R8: 22.5 over 29–33k).

Interpretation

Whole-board kernels help a single block (+31.1 over fatty) but do not replace depth (−112.4 against three blocks). One seed. Fatconv was continued past 33k (E-0008).

Takeaway

At equal budget, one 15×15 block @98 beat one 7×7 block @216 by about 30 pElo (one seed, just above seed noise) but trailed three 7×7 blocks by about 110.
