Looser fixed gradient caps (2.0 and 5.0) vs B-silu's step-20,600 blowup

gradient-clippingsilulr-scheduletraining-stabilityexact-resumebasic24

Question

E-0020 showed that an exact rerun of B-silu from its 18k checkpoint with grad_clip_max_norm 1.0 avoided the step-20,600 blowup that its own cap 15 reproduced bit for bit. A fixed 1.0 cap cannot be used everywhere: gNorm is about 30 at step 1 and 1–3 for the first few hundred steps, so it would clip nearly every early step. How loose can a fixed cap be and still avoid the blowup? This bounds the size of the unlogged precursor step and tells us whether a cap tied to the recent median is enough.

Interpretation

A cap that never binds leaves the run identical to B-silu bit for bit (ctl15), so clip5 leaving B-silu's path at 19,800 means some step in 19,751–19,799 had a pre-clip norm above 5: more than 13× the 0.33–0.38 logged around it, invisible on the 50-step lines. Any cap from 1 to 5 cut that step and the run never blew up, so the cause was one step far outside the normal range, not a gradual rise. Because every capped run left B-silu's path at 19,800, their batches at 20,600 met different weights and none logged a spike there; this shows the caps avoided the blowup, not what a cap of 2 or 5 would have done at 20,600 itself. The three caps differ by 7.2–50.8 pElo per probe with no consistent order, and at 23k all are within 12.8 of ReLU B. One start state, one seed, four probes per arm.

Takeaway

Exact reruns of B-silu from 18k with grad_clip_max_norm 2.0 and 5.0 both left B-silu's path at 19,800, exactly where the 1.0 cap did, and neither blew up (21k pElo 1373.2 and 1353.6 vs 457.4; 0 parked channels; 1560.6 and 1567.3 at 23k vs ReLU B's 1573.4). So B-silu had one unlogged step with a pre-clip norm above 5, more than 13x its usual 0.33-0.38, and any cap from 1 to 5 cut it. A cap at about 3x the recent median catches such a step without binding in normal training: the relative gradient cap, now on main, defaults to log only until its validation runs pass.
