Policy tail in bf16: accuracy now, and logit growth over long runs

numericspolicy-headbf16

Question

Our runs compute the policy tail in fp32 (fp32_from_pre_bn). The app default, mixed_final_projection, runs the tail's BN, ReLU and final 1×1 projection in bf16 and widens only the projection's output. Does that rounding cost anything, and could logits grow large enough over 500k–1M steps for it to matter?

Setup

Models
33k checkpoints of fatconv, R7, R8 (as E-0009).
Method
--probe-model --probe-set wide on build 2320, once with each --policy-tail-precision; growth from the dashboards' pLogit_mean history and the probes' policy_logit_abs_max.
Note
probe_loop.sh passes no tail flag, so its probes use the build's default tail: mixed on builds 2275 and later.

Interpretation

At |logit| ≈ 17 bf16's step is 0.125 against a legal spread of ~0.58, and the probes show no effect. The old runs' growth came from a drifting shared offset: the 2026-09-28 survey found legal logits at −120 … −266 on v5's later segments and qeu8's late line (Ejp0), against +9 … +19 on healthy lines. The current loss removes that drift (logits are centered per position before the cross-entropy, so that direction gets zero gradient). What remains is the legal-vs-illegal gap, which grows only logarithmically as illegal mass shrinks. Whether bf16 would matter at 32–64 is an inference from its spacing, not measured. Watch policy_logit_abs_max in long runs; past ~30 re-run this comparison.

Takeaway

Running the policy tail (BN, ReLU and final projection) in bf16 costs nothing measurable at 33k (≤ 2.1 pElo); bf16 spacing becomes comparable to the legal spread only around |logit| 32–64.
