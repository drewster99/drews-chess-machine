Training speed: fp32 vs mixed policy tail, and nt8y's step cost

speednumericspolicy-headbf16

Question

The mixed tail was made the app default for speed, but its saving had only been measured on Ejp0, whose policy head has a 512-channel pre-conv, where it saved ~5%. What does it save on our 128-channel heads? The same alone-on-the-GPU method also measured nt8y's step cost on this Mac (for E-0015).

Setup

Nets
R7 at 33k; the SE scale+bias seed-2 net at 7,282 steps; a fresh v4_5block_7x7 (20261005-3-SQ2a); nt8y's last checkpoint (ModelID 20260708-4-kEiZ, fp32 tail only).
Build / machine
2320 (1ab52554), M5 Max on AC power, one job on the GPU at a time (each run refuses to start otherwise).
Method
600 corpus-replay steps per run, A-B-B-A (fp32, mixed, mixed, fp32); wall time from step 200 to 550, plus the median of the logged per-step times.

R7 mixed B (832.3) is excluded: a CPU-only analysis (E-0013) ran during its timed window. Its median step time (747.5 ms) matches mixed A's (741.9). On median step times the changes are −1.5%, −0.6% and +0.3%. An earlier attempt that shared the GPU with a second benchmark is discarded.

Interpretation

With a 128-channel pre-conv there is little head work for bf16 to save; every difference is about the size of the fp32 A–B spread (R7 1.0%), so all three are within run-to-run noise. It is not worth the extra rounding (E-0010) for training these nets. nt8y runs at 443 ms/step (wall) here, 1.83× faster than R7; its July logs imply ~2.4× (wall ~335 ms/step), a gap not explained (E-0015).

Takeaway

The mixed (bf16) policy tail changes training step time by only −1.5% to +0.3% on 128-channel heads, within run-to-run noise, so our runs keep passing --policy-tail-precision fp32_from_pre_bn (the app default is still mixed).
