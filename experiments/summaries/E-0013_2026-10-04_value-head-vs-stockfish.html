Value head vs Stockfish at predicting game results

value-headstockfishevaluation

Question

The value head's cross-entropy against game results is only ~7% below always guessing the result rates (0.763 vs 0.821). Is the head weak, or are results simply that unpredictable? Compare with a strong evaluator on the same positions.

Setup

Model
R7 at 33k, 20261002-bench_v5s3_noSE_noReZero-replay-step33000 (ModelID 20261002-2-5tKN).
Positions
The 8,172 corpus positions of E-0009 (shards 20/26/32/38, two random plies per game, seed 20261004); target = the game's result from the side to move.
Stockfish
Stockfish dev-20260529-b1053e60, 50,000 nodes per position, 1 thread. Its own W/D/L model expects engine-level play (CE 3.07 used raw), so its eval was mapped to these games' results by a 3-class logistic fit on one feature, tanh(cp/400), 2-fold cross-validated (fitted on one half, scored on the other, both ways).
Measure
Cross-entropy (nats) against the actual result; e−CE is the typical (geometric-mean) probability given to it.

Interpretation

A much stronger evaluator, mapped through a single stage-blind feature, gets no lower CE on these labels, which suggests most of the remaining 0.76 is outcome noise: later blunders, flags and resignations in Lichess human and bot games. The one-feature mapping is miscalibrated late in games (ply 101+: 0.936 vs our 0.668), so a richer mapping could do somewhat better. The head learns where it should: no better than the base rate in the opening, clearly better late in games and with lopsided material (where it beats Stockfish's mapped eval: 0.626 vs 0.690). Even in the last 10 plies both sit near 0.65 (~52% on the actual result). Material is a simple side-to-move piece count, blurred by mid-exchange positions.

Takeaway

Our value head predicts these games' results as well as a recalibrated Stockfish eval (CE 0.763 vs 0.771), so most of what is left is outcome noise, not a weak head.
