How our long runs were trained: LR, momentum and heads

lr-schedulehistorypolicy-headvalue-head

Question

Before changing the LR schedule for a long run: what did the long corpus-replay runs that kept improving actually use?

Setup

Runs
v5 (5 × 7×7 @128, 8.45M), qeu8 (2 × 15×15 @64, 3.93M), nt8y (3 × 15×15 @32, 1.53M), coxw (1 × 7×7 @128, 1.86M); all corpus replay of 20260624-192615-w3aA5b, June–August 2026 builds (the logs carry no build numbers).
Method
First [REPLAY-HPARAMS] line and every logged step LR of each surviving segment log; head shapes from the dashboard registry.
Gaps
Logs lost for v5's first three segments (0–100k), nt8y's first segment (0–65,883) and coxw's (0–55,550). v5's are recorded in documentation/v5-layernorm-output.md (LR 0.01 flat, 500-step warmup, momentum 0.9 then 0.93, weight decay 1e-4 → 5e-4 → 2.5e-4, clip 30, buffer 1M); nt8y's and coxw's first segments are unrecorded.

Interpretation

Every logged step after warmup in every surviving log reads exactly lr=0.01; no segment ran an LR cycle. The cycle used in the 33k tests swings the effective step 0.016–0.67, around the long runs' constant 0.10–0.14. The two best runs used opposite head balances (v5 small policy and big value head; qeu8 big policy and small value head), but their trunks also differ, so the head's own effect is not separable here. They peaked within 30 pElo; v5 later declined (800–860k mean 1557) while qeu8 held (1656).

Takeaway

Our two best long runs (v5 peak 1770.5, qeu8 peak 1742.1) trained at a constant LR of 0.01 with no cycle, and used opposite head sizes.
